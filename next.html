Embedded Systems

An embedded system is a dedicated computer system designed for one or two specific control functions within a larger mechanical or electrical system. Unlike a general-purpose PC, it is built to do one thing exceptionally well, often subject to real-time computing constraints.

Anatomy of an Embedded System

Inside a typical microcontroller-based device.

1

Sensors

Measure physical stimuli (light, heat, motion).

2

Input I/F

ADC converts analog signals to digital data.

3

MCU

CPU, RAM, Flash on one chip processing logic.

4

Output I/F

PWM, DAC, and digital signals to drive loads.

5

Actuators

Motors, heaters, valves, displays, LEDs.

Real-World Example: Washing Machine

Sensors detect water level → MCU executes wash program → Motor spins & Heater warms water.
